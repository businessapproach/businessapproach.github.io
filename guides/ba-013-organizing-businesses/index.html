01

Label Setups

~$35 to start

You go through a room that's already sorted, or one you sort together, and add clear, matching labels to bins, shelves, jars and drawers so everyone in the house knows where things go. It's a quick, satisfying finish that clients love to show off, and it pairs well with every other idea on this list.

Good fit if: you like neat, consistent details and don't mind small, careful work.

Your starter kit

  • Brother PT-H110 label maker — ~$35 (Brother · See options on Amazon)

Label tape is a refill cost, so check how many labels a job needs and include tape in your price. Agree on wording and style with the client before you print a whole room's worth.

What customers pay pros

Thumbtack puts the average professional organizer rate at $65/hour, with an average range of $60-$75/hour (low end $35-$45/hour). A full organizing job costs $227-$640 nationally, and most people pay around $380. A label-only visit is usually shorter, so many beginners price it as a small flat add-on. (Thumbtack)

As a beginner, quote below that range until you have reviews and photos of your work.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Label your own pantry or closet, take a clean photo, and post it in your neighborhood Facebook group with a simple starter price.
  2. Offer a free labeling finish to the first few clients who book any other organizing job, in exchange for a review.
  3. Ask busy parents and new movers you know; labels help kids and roommates put things back.
Watch out: ask before labeling anything permanent, and use removable labels on renters' cabinets and on furniture with a delicate finish. Never throw away or move items you're labeling without the owner's OK.

Toy Declutter

~$31 to start

You help parents sort toys into keep, donate and toss piles, group what's left by type, and put it into bins kids can reach. Many families call before birthdays and the holidays, when new toys are about to arrive.

Good fit if: you're patient, upbeat and comfortable working around kids and their stuff.

Your starter kit

  • Sorbus 6-pack foldable fabric storage cubes — ~$31 (Walmart · See options on Amazon)

Measure the client's shelves or cube unit before buying bins, so everything fits. Agree up front whether you bring bins or the family buys them.

What customers pay pros

Thumbtack's data puts the national average cost of a personal organizer at $227-$640, and most people pay around $380. The average hourly rate is $65/hour, with an average range of $60-$75/hour. (Thumbtack)

As a beginner, quote below that range until you have reviews and photos of your work.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Offer a discounted playroom reset to two or three parents you know, and ask for before-and-after photos (no kids in them) plus a review.
  2. Post in local parent groups a few weeks before the holidays with a simple "make room for new toys" package.
  3. Offer to drop the donate pile at a local charity as part of the price; parents love not having to do that step.
Watch out: always let the parent (and the child, where it's appropriate) make the final call on what goes. Don't donate toys with missing parts, recalls or broken pieces, and keep small parts away from little kids while you work.
03

Closet Refresh

~$22 to start

You pull everything out of a closet, help the client decide what stays, then rehang it by type and color on slim matching hangers so more fits and it's easy to see. A closet refresh is quick to show off, which makes it a great first service.

Good fit if: you like fashion or just love a tidy row of hangers, and you can be honest but kind about what to let go.

Your starter kit

  • SONGMICS 50-pack velvet hangers — ~$22 (Walmart · See options on Amazon)

Count the client's hanging clothes first; a big closet may need more than one pack. Velvet hangers are slim, so they also free up rail space.

What customers pay pros

Taskrabbit's organization cost guide lists closet organization at $150-$2,000, and says organizers cost $34 per hour on average in the U.S. Supplies like hangers ($3-$25 per pack) are not included in service fees. (Taskrabbit)

As a beginner, quote below that range until you have reviews and photos of your work.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Refresh your own closet or a friend's, take a before and after photo from the same spot, and post it with a starter price.
  2. Offer a bundle with drop-off of donated clothes, so the client doesn't have to deal with the bags.
  3. Ask each happy client to tag you or mention you in their local group; closets are easy to share.
Watch out: never toss or donate clothes without the client's clear OK on each bag. Use a sturdy step stool, not a chair, for top shelves, and lift heavy bins with your legs, not your back.

Drawer Sorting

~$20 to start

You empty a drawer, help the client keep only what they use, and fit dividers so every item has a place. Kitchen junk drawers, utensil drawers, bathroom drawers and dressers are all good starting jobs, and they're small enough to do several in one visit.

Good fit if: you're detail-minded, like quick wins, and enjoy fitting things together.

Your starter kit

  • Royal Craft Wood bamboo drawer dividers, 4-pack (gray) — ~$20 (Walmart · See options on Amazon)

Measure the drawer depth and width before you buy; adjustable dividers need to match the drawer size. A tape measure is the only other tool you need.

What customers pay pros

Taskrabbit says home organization services cost $34 per hour on average in the U.S. Thumbtack's average professional organizer rate is $65/hour, with a low-end range of $35-$45/hour. Drawer jobs are short, so many beginners charge per drawer or bundle several into one visit. (Taskrabbit)

As a beginner, quote below that range until you have reviews and photos of your work.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Post a before-and-after of your own junk drawer with an offer like "three drawers, one flat price".
  2. Offer it as an easy first booking to people who aren't ready for a whole-room job.
  3. Ask happy clients which room drives them crazy next; drawer clients often book the closet or pantry after.
Watch out: watch for sharp knives, tools and medications in junk and bathroom drawers. Hand medications and anything that looks important, like keys or documents, to the owner instead of sorting them yourself.
05

Pantry Setup

~$21 to start

You take everything out of a pantry, check dates with the client, group what's left (snacks, baking, canned goods, breakfast) and put it into clear bins so it's easy to see what's there. Pantries are popular before the holidays, when people stock up for guests.

Good fit if: you like order, don't mind a bit of wiping down, and enjoy a big visual before-and-after.

Your starter kit

  • Sindcom 6-pack clear storage bins with lids — ~$21 (Walmart · See options on Amazon)

Measure shelf depth and height before buying bins. Pairing this with label setups makes the result look finished.

What customers pay pros

Taskrabbit's organization cost guide lists pantry organization at $200-$750, with organizers costing $34 per hour on average in the U.S. It notes that supplies such as plastic bins ($5-$30+ each) and food containers ($5-$25+) are not included in service fees. (Taskrabbit)

As a beginner, quote below that range until you have reviews and photos of your work.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Do your own pantry or a friend's first, and post the before-and-after in your neighborhood group a few weeks before Thanksgiving or the holidays.
  2. Offer a "holiday-ready pantry" package with a set number of bins included.
  3. Ask happy clients for a short review and a referral to one friend who's hosting this year.
Watch out: let the client decide what to throw out, even past-date items. Ask about food allergies in the home before you handle food, keep allergen items in their own bin, and don't mix foods out of their original packaging without the owner's OK.
06

Decor Storage

~$30 to start

You help clients pack away holiday decorations so they're safe, labeled and easy to find next year: ornaments in slotted storage, lights wound neatly, and each box marked by room or theme. The busy times are right after the holidays and when decorations come out again.

Good fit if: you're careful with fragile things and like seasonal work you can book again every year.

Your starter kit

  • Whitmor 64-compartment ornament storage cube — ~$30 (Walmart · See options on Amazon)

Ask how many ornaments the client has before you buy; large collections may need more than one box. Wrap very fragile or sentimental pieces in tissue before they go in.

What to charge

We couldn't find a national cost guide for holiday decor storage on its own; it's usually billed as general organizing time. For context, Taskrabbit says home organization costs $34 per hour on average in the U.S., and Thumbtack puts the average professional organizer rate at $65/hour. Check what local organizers charge, and price a simple flat visit or hourly rate with storage boxes billed at what you paid. See Taskrabbit's organization cost guide.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Post in your neighborhood group in late December with an offer to pack up decorations after the holidays.
  2. Offer to book each client again for next year's unpacking at the same visit.
  3. Ask older neighbors and busy families you know; climbing to attics and lifting boxes is the part people dread.
Watch out: use a sturdy ladder with someone holding it for attics and high shelves, and don't carry heavy boxes up or down stairs alone. Check strings of lights for damaged wires and tell the owner instead of packing them. Handle sentimental pieces gently and photograph anything fragile before packing.

Before you take your first job

  1. Check local rules. Search "[your city] business license" to see whether you need one for a home-service business. Rules vary by city and state, even for part-time work.
  2. Pick one room type. Start with closets, pantries or drawers, get a few good reviews, then add the rest.
  3. Put it in writing. Send a short message with the room, time, price and who buys the bins or hangers before every job.
  4. Take before and after photos. Ask permission, shoot from the same spot both times, and keep family photos, addresses and kids out of the frame.
  5. Track every dollar. Side income is taxable, so record what you earn and spend, keep supply receipts, and set some aside.
Pro tip

Take a before photo of every space. One reveal post books the next client.

FAQ

Which of these is cheapest to start?

Drawer sorting, at about $20 for a 4-pack of bamboo dividers. Pantry setup (about $21) and closet refresh (about $22) are close behind.

Do I need a business license?

It depends on your city and state. Many places require a general business license for paid home services, even part-time. Check your city's website or call the clerk's office before you start.

How much can I make?

We don't promise earnings. Where we show prices, they're what customers typically pay professional organizers, from published cost guides. As a beginner, expect to charge less until you have reviews and before-and-after photos.

Who pays for the bins and hangers?

Agree on it before the job. Many organizers either ask the client to buy supplies from a list, or buy them and bill the client exactly what they paid, with receipts.

Can teens do these?

Closet, drawer, pantry and toy jobs can work for teens helping family friends, with a parent's OK and an adult home during the visit. Leave ladder and attic work to adults, and check your state's rules for working minors.
