Vinyl Decals

~$99 to start

You design and cut vinyl decals people stick on mugs, tumblers, water bottles, laptops, car windows and storage jars. Custom names and short phrases are popular gifts, and you can bring a few ready-made designs to every fair.

Good fit if: you like design, are comfortable learning a simple app, and enjoy careful, detailed work.

Your starter kit

  • Cricut Joy 2 cutting machine — ~$99 (Cricut · See options on Amazon)
  • Permanent and removable vinyl — price varies (craft stores)

The post's ~$99 covers the machine only. You'll also need vinyl and transfer tape; start with a few colors and buy more once you know what sells.

What to charge

There's no national price guide for handmade decals. Before your first market, look up what similar custom decals with good reviews sell for on Etsy and at local fairs, and add up your vinyl and transfer tape cost for each one. A common rule of thumb, and the tip from our post, is to price at about 3 times your material cost so booth fees and your time are covered.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Make custom name decals for a few friends' mugs or bottles and ask them to share photos.
  2. Bring a small set of ready-made designs plus a sign saying you take custom orders for pickup.
  3. Offer matching decals to local teams, clubs or teachers for end-of-season gifts.
Watch out: never cut sports team logos, cartoon characters, brand names or other people's artwork; selling them can break copyright and trademark rules. Stick to your own designs, plain names and fonts that allow commercial use. Keep the cutting blade out of reach of kids.

Soy Candles

~$52 to start

You pour soy wax candles in tins or jars, add a scent and a simple label, and sell them as gifts. Seasonal scents and small gift-size tins tend to move well at holiday markets.

Good fit if: you're patient, like testing and tweaking, and are careful around hot wax.

Your starter kit

  • Hearth & Harbor soy candle making kit (wax, pot, wicks, tins) — ~$52 (Walmart · See options on Amazon)

Test-burn a few candles from every batch at home before you sell any, so you know the wick size and scent strength work.

What to charge

There's no national price guide for handmade candles. Check what similar-size soy candles sell for at local fairs and on Etsy, and work out your cost per candle (wax, wick, tin, scent and label). A common rule of thumb, and the tip from our post, is to price at about 3 times your material cost so booth fees and your time are covered.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Give a few test candles to friends and family and ask for honest feedback on scent and burn.
  2. Offer a small gift set (three tins) at your first fair; sets are easy holiday gifts.
  3. Ask local coffee shops or boutiques if they'd stock a few candles on a shelf.
Watch out: melt wax over low, steady heat, never leave it unattended, and keep a lid nearby in case of a flare-up. Put a clear safety label on every candle (never leave burning unattended, keep away from kids, pets and things that can catch fire, trim the wick). Only test-burn candles where you can watch them the whole time.

Door Wreaths

~$3 to start

You build seasonal door wreaths on a wire frame using faux greenery, ribbon, bows and small ornaments. It's the cheapest idea on this list to start, and one good wreath on your own door is free advertising.

Good fit if: you have a good eye for color, like hands-on work, and enjoy decorating for the seasons.

Your starter kit

  • Hobby Lobby 12 in round metal wire wreath frame — ~$3 (Hobby Lobby · See options on Amazon)
  • Faux greenery, ribbon and decorations — price varies (craft stores)

The post's ~$3 is the frame only. Greenery and ribbon are where most of the cost is, so watch for craft store sales and buy for a few wreaths at a time.

What to charge

There's no national price guide for handmade wreaths. Add up the frame, greenery, ribbon and extras for each wreath, and check what similar wreaths sell for at local fairs and on Etsy. A common rule of thumb, and the tip from our post, is to price at about 3 times your material cost so booth fees and your time are covered.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Hang your best wreath on your own front door and post a photo in your neighborhood group.
  2. Offer custom colors (school colors, a favorite team's colors without logos, wedding colors) as made-to-order wreaths.
  3. Take orders at your first fair for the next season, like spring wreaths, and collect a small deposit.
Watch out: use hot glue guns carefully and keep them away from kids. Wreaths with real greenery or dried flowers can be a fire risk near candles or heaters, so faux materials are a safer start. Bulky wreaths need a sturdy box for transport so they don't get crushed.

Glycerin Soap

~$46 to start

You make melt-and-pour soap: melt a ready-made glycerin base, add color and scent, pour it into molds and let it set. There's no lye to handle, which makes it a good beginner option, and wrapped bars make easy small gifts.

Good fit if: you like simple, repeatable projects and enjoy playing with colors and scents.

Your starter kit

  • FASTTOBUY soap making kit with electric melting pot — ~$46 (Walmart · See options on Amazon)

Let bars set fully and wrap them tightly (for example in shrink wrap or cello bags) so glycerin soap doesn't sweat before the fair.

What to charge

There's no national price guide for handmade soap. Work out your cost per bar (base, scent, color, wrap and label), and check what similar bars sell for at local fairs and on Etsy. A common rule of thumb, and the tip from our post, is to price at about 3 times your material cost so booth fees and your time are covered.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Give sample bars to friends and ask which scents they'd buy again.
  2. Sell bundles of three bars at your fair; bundles make easy gifts and raise each sale.
  3. Offer custom-scent or custom-color bars as party favors or wedding favors.
Watch out: how you describe your soap matters. The FDA says claims like moisturizing or deodorizing can make a soap a cosmetic, and claims like treating acne or killing germs can make it a drug, each with its own rules. Keep labels simple, list the ingredients, and never promise health benefits. Melted base is hot, so keep kids away from the pot.

Crochet Gifts

~$19 to start

You crochet small, quick gifts like coasters, scrunchies, mini plushies, hats and ornaments. Small items you can finish in an evening sell better at fairs than big pieces that take weeks.

Good fit if: you already crochet or are willing to practice, and like having a project in your hands while you relax.

Your starter kit

  • Yarniss 55-piece beginner crochet kit (12 yarns, 10 hooks) — ~$19 (Walmart · See options on Amazon)

Free beginner tutorials online are plenty to learn the basic stitches. Practice on a few pieces you keep before you make items to sell.

What to charge

There's no national price guide for handmade crochet items. Time yourself on each piece, add up the yarn you use, and check what similar items sell for at local fairs and on Etsy. A common rule of thumb, and the tip from our post, is to price at about 3 times your material cost, but crochet takes time, so make sure the price also covers your hours.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Make a small set of quick gifts and post photos in local craft and holiday market groups.
  2. Take custom color orders at your fair for pickup or delivery later.
  3. Pair up with a friend who sells another craft and share a booth to split the fee.
Watch out: items meant for children under 12, like toys and baby items, fall under federal children's product safety rules, so read the Consumer Product Safety Commission's guidance before you make or sell them. Avoid small buttons or safety eyes on anything for babies, since they can be a choking risk. Only sell your own designs or patterns that allow selling finished items.

Ornaments

~$24 to start

You fill and decorate clear ornaments with things like glitter, faux snow, mini pom-poms, photos or names, and sell them as personalized gifts. Names and dates (like a baby's first Christmas) are popular custom orders.

Good fit if: you like quick, colorful projects and enjoy making personalized gifts.

Your starter kit

  • Juvale clear fillable ornaments, 24-pack — ~$24 (Michaels · See options on Amazon)
  • Fillers, paint pens or glitter — price varies (craft stores)

A 24-pack gives you enough for practice pieces plus a first batch to sell. Plastic ornaments are lighter and safer to transport than glass.

What to charge

There's no national price guide for handmade ornaments. Work out your cost per ornament (the ornament plus fillers, paint and ribbon), and check what similar personalized ornaments sell for at local fairs and on Etsy. A common rule of thumb, and the tip from our post, is to price at about 3 times your material cost so booth fees and your time are covered.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Make personalized ornaments for your own family and share photos (with permission) in local groups.
  2. Bring blank and finished samples to your fair and offer names added while customers wait.
  3. Take custom orders early in the season, since personalized ornaments sell best before mid-December.
Watch out: double-check the spelling of every name before you start, and confirm it with the customer in writing. Keep glitter, small fillers and paint pens away from young kids, and seal ornament caps well so nothing spills out.

Before you take your first job

  1. Check local rules. Search "[your city] business license" and ask the fair organizer whether you need a sales tax permit or seller's permit. Rules vary by city and state.
  2. Pick one craft. Start with the one you already enjoy, make a small batch, and see what sells before buying more supplies.
  3. Know your cost per item. Add up materials for each piece so your prices cover them, your booth fee and your time.
  4. Take payment simply. Bring some cash for change and set up a card reader or payment app you already know, and give a receipt when asked.
  5. Track every dollar. Keep receipts for supplies and booth fees; side income is taxable, so set some aside.

FAQ

Which of these is cheapest to start?

Door wreaths, at about $3 for a 12 inch wire frame, though you'll also need greenery and ribbon. Crochet gifts are next at about $19 for a beginner kit.

Do I need a business license or sales tax permit?

It depends on your city and state. Many places require a business license, and many states require a seller's permit to collect sales tax at fairs. Ask the fair organizer and check your city and state websites before your first market.

How much can I make?

We don't promise earnings. Sales depend on the fair, the season and your prices. Start with a small batch, track what sells, and adjust from there.

Can I sell these online too?

Yes. Many makers add an Etsy shop after their first fairs. Etsy charges a $0.20 listing fee and a 6.5% transaction fee on each sale, so build those into your prices.

Can teens do these?

Crochet, ornaments and wreaths can work for teens with a parent's OK. Candles and soap involve hot wax and melted base, so an adult should supervise. Check your state's rules for working minors.
