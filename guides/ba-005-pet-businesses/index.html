Holiday travel is when pet owners scramble the most: they need someone to walk the dog, feed the cat, drive a pet to the groomer or tidy up after a shedding season. If you love animals and you're reliable, you can start helping with one of these services for less than $20, and pet sitting costs nothing to start.

Below are the six ideas from our post, with the starter item we priced on a real store page, what customers pay professionals where we found a reliable published number, and the simplest way to land your first three customers. Pets are family to their owners, so trust is everything: start small, show up on time and keep owners updated.

01

Dog Walking

~$13 to start

You walk dogs on a regular schedule, usually 30 or 60 minutes at a time, while their owners are at work or away. Many walkers also refill water bowls, pick up after the dog and send a quick photo update after each walk.

Good fit if: you're active, love dogs, and can commit to the same time slots every week.

Your starter kit

  • Vibrant Life 6 ft adjustable hands-free leash — ~$13 (Walmart · See options on Amazon)

Bring poop bags and a little water on every walk. Use the owner's own collar or harness, and check it's snug before you leave the house.

What customers pay pros

Thumbtack says the average cost of a dog walker ranges from $20 to $40 per hour, depending on the length of the walk, the number of dogs and your location. One Thumbtack pro it profiles charges $20 for a 30-minute walk. (Thumbtack)

As a beginner, quote below that range until you have reviews and photos of your work.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Walk a friend's or neighbor's dog for free for a week, and ask for an honest review you can share.
  2. Post a simple offer (walk length, price, area you cover) in your neighborhood Facebook group or on Nextdoor.
  3. Leave a flyer at local apartment buildings, vet offices and pet supply stores that allow them.
Watch out: start with one dog at a time, and only walk dogs together if they live in the same home. Meet each dog with its owner first, ask about pulling, other dogs and anything it's scared of, and keep the leash on at all times. Get the owner's emergency contact and vet details before the first walk.

Nail Trims

~$20 to start

You trim dogs' nails at the owner's home, a quick job many owners dread doing themselves. It's a short visit, so you can fit several into one afternoon in the same neighborhood.

Good fit if: you're calm and steady with animals and are willing to learn the technique properly before charging.

Your starter kit

  • Safari Professional nail trimmer, large — ~$20 (Walmart · See options on Amazon)

Keep styptic powder on hand in case you cut a nail too short, and ask a groomer or vet tech to show you how to find the quick before your first paid job.

What customers pay pros

Thumbtack says the average cost to get your dog's nails trimmed usually ranges from approximately $10 to $25. Its example price list for mobile grooming shows Nail trim: $15-$25. (Thumbtack)

As a beginner, quote below that range until you have reviews and photos of your work.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Practice on dogs belonging to friends and family whose owners can help hold them.
  2. Offer a nail-trim add-on to people you already walk or sit for.
  3. Post a flat in-home price in local pet owner groups, mentioning that you come to them.
Watch out: never force a scared or aggressive dog; stop and suggest a groomer or vet instead. Have the owner help hold the dog, trim a little at a time, and keep treats handy. Don't offer anything medical like treating injured nails.
03

Pet Sitting

Free to start

You care for pets in their own home while the owners are away: feeding, fresh water, litter boxes or potty breaks, play time and a photo update. Some sitters also stay overnight, which owners book for longer trips.

Good fit if: you're responsible, live near your customers, and can keep a strict visit schedule even on holidays.

Your starter kit

  • Google Business Profile listing — Free (Google)

We left Rover off the list on purpose: it isn't free to join, because it charges a one-time profile review fee. A free Google Business Profile lets local owners find you and leave reviews.

What customers pay pros

Thumbtack says on average, pet sitters charge $27-$30 per hour, and the national average cost for overnight pet sitting services ranges from $160-$268. (Thumbtack)

As a beginner, quote below that range until you have reviews and photos of your work.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Tell friends, neighbors and coworkers you're available over the holidays, since that's when owners scramble for sitters.
  2. Set up your free Google Business Profile and ask every happy customer to leave a review there.
  3. Offer a free meet-and-greet before the first booking so the pet and owner get to know you.
Watch out: get written care instructions, the vet's number and an emergency contact for every pet, and keep house keys secure and unlabeled. Only take on as many visits as you can do on time, and never leave a pet without care if your plans change; have a backup sitter the owner has approved.

Pet Taxi

~$13 to start

You drive pets to and from vet visits, grooming appointments, daycare and boarding when their owners can't. Some owners ride along; others hand you the leash or carrier and meet you at the appointment.

Good fit if: you have a reliable car, a clean driving record, and a calm way with nervous animals.

Your starter kit

  • IRIS animal hammock seat cover — ~$13 (Walmart · See options on Amazon)

Ask owners to provide their pet's own crate, carrier or car harness, so every pet rides secured and in gear that fits.

What to charge

We could not find a reliable national price guide for this one. Search your ZIP code on Thumbtack or Nextdoor and price yourself just under local listings.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Ask local groomers and vet offices if you can leave a card for clients who don't drive or work during the day.
  2. Offer rides to older neighbors' pets; many seniors no longer drive to the vet.
  3. Post a simple per-trip price for your area in local pet owner groups, and mention wait time is extra.
Watch out: using your car for paid trips may not be covered by a personal auto policy, so ask your insurance agent before you start. Never leave a pet alone in the car, keep every pet secured in a crate, carrier or harness, and only drive one household's pets at a time.

Dog Bathing

~$18 to start

You give dogs a bath at the owner's home: brush out loose fur, shampoo, rinse well, towel dry and brush again. It's a simpler service than full grooming, with no haircuts, which makes it a good place to start.

Good fit if: you don't mind getting wet, you're patient with wiggly dogs, and you like seeing a clean, happy pet at the end.

Your starter kit

  • Burt's Bees oatmeal dog shampoo, 16 oz — ~$9 (Walmart · See options on Amazon)
  • Vibrant Life bathing palm brush — ~$9 (Walmart · See options on Amazon)

The post's ~$18 total is the shampoo and the palm brush together. Ask the owner for old towels, and use their tub or an outdoor hose only with warm water in good weather.

What customers pay pros

In Thumbtack's example list of pet grooming prices, a bath costs $25-$50, depending on dog size. (Thumbtack)

As a beginner, quote below that range until you have reviews and photos of your work.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Bathe the dogs of two or three friends and take before-and-after photos (with permission).
  2. Offer a bath add-on to your dog walking or pet sitting customers.
  3. Post a simple price by dog size (small, medium, large) in local groups.
Watch out: keep shampoo away from eyes and ears, test the water temperature, and use a non-slip mat so dogs don't slip. Large dogs are heavy, so don't lift them alone. If you notice skin sores, lumps or fleas, tell the owner and suggest they check with their vet.

Fur Removal

~$17 to start

You remove pet hair from couches, rugs, car seats and pet beds, the stubborn fur a vacuum misses. A rubber-blade broom pulls embedded hair out of carpet and upholstery fast, and it pairs well with a quick tidy-up.

Good fit if: you like visible, satisfying results and don't mind a dusty, hairy job.

Your starter kit

  • Casabella rubber blade broom — ~$17 (Target · See options on Amazon)

Test the broom on a hidden spot first, and ask the owner before using it on delicate fabrics like velvet or silk.

What to charge

We could not find a reliable national price guide for this one. Search your ZIP code on Thumbtack or Nextdoor and price yourself just under local listings.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Do a free couch or car seat for a friend with a shedding dog and take before-and-after photos of the fur you pulled out.
  2. Offer it as a pre-holiday-guests service in local groups: a fur-free living room before family arrives.
  3. Add it on for your pet sitting and dog walking customers, since you're already in their home.
Watch out: wear a dust mask if you have allergies, and ask about any pets that might slip out while doors are open. Stick to removing hair; deep upholstery cleaning and stain removal with chemicals are a different job.

Before you take your first job

  1. Check local rules. Search "[your city] business license" to see whether you need one for a pet care business. Rules vary by city and state.
  2. Think about insurance. Ask a local agent about pet sitter or general liability insurance, and about business use of your car if you'll drive pets.
  3. Do a meet-and-greet. Meet every pet with its owner before the first booking, and write down feeding, health and emergency details.
  4. Put it in writing. Send a short summary of the service, schedule and price before every job so there are no surprises.
  5. Track every dollar. Keep receipts for supplies; side income is taxable, so set some aside.
Pro tip

Offer a free meet-and-greet before the first booking. Owners book who their pet already knows.

FAQ

Which of these is cheapest to start?

Pet sitting, since a Google Business Profile listing is free. Dog walking and pet taxi are next at about $13 each, for a hands-free leash or a hammock seat cover.

Do I need a business license?

It depends on where you live. Many cities require a general business license for paid services, even part-time. Check your city's website or call the clerk's office before you start.

How much can I make?

We don't promise earnings. The prices above are what customers pay professionals, from published cost guides. As a beginner, expect to charge less until you have reviews.

Do I need to join an app like Rover?

No. Rover charges a one-time profile review fee, so we left it off. You can start with friends, neighbors, local groups and a free Google Business Profile, and add apps later if you want.

Can teens do these?

Dog walking, pet sitting and fur removal can work for responsible teens with a parent's OK, starting with pets they know. Pet taxi needs a licensed driver. Check your state's rules for working minors.
