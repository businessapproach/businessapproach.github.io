Cleaning is one of the most reliable small businesses there is. People always need dirty things made clean, the results are easy to see, and you can start with a few tools and your weekends. You don't need a storefront, an app or a big ad budget, just good work and a way to show it.

Below are the six ideas from our post, with the starter kit we priced on real store pages, what customers typically pay professionals, and the simplest way to land your first three customers. Pick one that fits where you live and what you're comfortable doing, and get good at it before adding a second.

01

Pressure Washing

~$195 to start

You clean driveways, sidewalks, patios, fences and siding with a pressure washer, removing dirt, mildew and stains. Driveways and walkways are the best place to start: flat, ground-level and very visible results.

Good fit if: you like satisfying before-and-after work outdoors and are fine with getting wet.

Your starter kit

  • Briggs & Stratton 2000 PSI electric pressure washer — ~$195 (Home Depot · See options on Amazon)
  • Garden hose — price varies (any hardware store · See options on Amazon)

The post's ~$195 total is the electric washer; most customers already have a hose and outdoor spigot you can use (ask first).

What customers pay pros

Pressure washing prices range from $170 to $310 on average, with most homeowners spending around $210; per-area pricing runs about $0.35 to $0.77 per square foot. (HomeGuide)

As a beginner, quote below that range until you have reviews and photos of your work.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Wash half of your own driveway first and photograph the clean line between the two halves. That one photo sells the job.
  2. Post it in your neighborhood Facebook group or on Nextdoor with a simple flat price for a standard driveway.
  3. Offer to do the sidewalk of the house next door for a small add-on price while you're already set up.
Watch out: a pressure washer stream can cut skin, so never point it at people or pets, and wear closed shoes and eye protection. Start on concrete; soft wood, old siding and window seals can be damaged by high pressure. Check local rules about wash water going into storm drains.

Gutter Cleaning

~$140 to start

You scoop leaves and debris out of gutters, bag it, and check that downspouts drain. Fall and spring are the busy seasons, and many homeowners want it done every year.

Good fit if: you're comfortable and careful on a ladder and want a seasonal service with repeat customers.

Your starter kit

  • Werner 6 ft fiberglass step ladder — ~$129 (Home Depot · See options on Amazon)
  • Work gloves and a gutter scoop — price varies (any hardware store · See options on Amazon)

The post's ~$140 total is the ladder plus gloves and a plastic gutter scoop. A 6 ft ladder suits one-story homes only.

What customers pay pros

The average U.S. homeowner spends $167 on gutter cleaning, and most cleanings cost between $119 and $234, or about $0.95 to $1.25 per linear foot. (Angi)

As a beginner, quote below that range until you have reviews and photos of your work.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Clean your own or a family member's gutters and take photos of the full scoop of muck next to the clean gutter.
  2. Walk single-story neighborhoods with lots of trees and leave a flyer with a flat one-story price.
  3. Offer a spring return visit at a small discount so every fall customer becomes a repeat customer.
Watch out: stick to one-story homes, set the ladder on firm, level ground, never stand on the top step, and have someone with you. Stay well away from power lines, and don't work on wet or icy days.

Move-Out Cleaning

~$150 to start

You deep-clean empty homes and apartments so tenants get their deposit back or sellers can hand over a clean house: kitchens, bathrooms, inside cabinets, floors, baseboards and windowsills.

Good fit if: you're thorough, don't mind scrubbing, and want work that comes in steadily around the end of every month.

Your starter kit

  • RIDGID 5 gallon wet/dry shop vac — ~$119 (Home Depot · See options on Amazon)
  • Sponges, microfiber cloths and all-purpose spray — price varies (any store · See options on Amazon)

The post's ~$150 total is the shop vac plus sponges and an all-purpose spray. Add supplies as jobs come in.

What customers pay pros

Move-out cleaning costs $0.15 to $0.25 per square foot, or $150 to $500 total on average, typically $25 to $50 per hour, per person. (HomeGuide)

As a beginner, quote below that range until you have reviews and photos of your work.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Write a clear move-out checklist (inside oven, fridge, cabinets, baseboards) and share it with every quote. Landlords love checklists.
  2. Introduce yourself to small local landlords and property managers; they need empty units cleaned between tenants.
  3. Post in local moving, college and apartment groups near the end of each month, when leases end.
Watch out: never mix cleaning products (especially bleach with ammonia or acids), keep windows open and wear gloves. Agree in writing on what's included and do a walk-through with the client before and after.

Pet Waste Removal

~$30 to start

You visit yards on a regular schedule (usually weekly) and scoop, bag and dispose of dog waste. It's the cheapest idea on the list to start, and customers tend to stay on a weekly plan for a long time.

Good fit if: you're not squeamish, like being outside, and want simple, recurring weekly income.

Your starter kit

  • Ames pan & rake pooper scooper — ~$25 (Home Depot · See options on Amazon)
  • Sturdy trash bags — price varies (any store · See options on Amazon)

The post's ~$30 total is the scooper plus a box of sturdy bags.

What customers pay pros

Dog waste removal costs about $15 to $30 per week for one dog, $20 to $40 per visit every other week, and $60 to $150 for a one-time cleanup. (HomeGuide)

As a beginner, quote below that range until you have reviews and photos of your work.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Offer a one-time "spring/fall yard reset" to a few neighbors with dogs, then invite them onto a weekly plan.
  2. Ask local dog walkers, groomers and pet sitters if you can leave a card; they hear about busy owners first.
  3. Group customers on the same few streets so each route takes less driving.
Watch out: wear gloves, wash your hands well, and disinfect your scooper and shoes between yards so you don't spread germs or parasites from one yard to the next. Close every gate behind you and check where your town allows pet waste to be disposed of.
05

Garage Cleanouts

~$140 to start

You help customers sort what to keep, donate and toss, then sweep and vacuum the garage and put things back neatly. You're selling a weekend of hard work they don't want to do.

Good fit if: you're organized, can lift and carry, and enjoy turning a mess into an orderly space.

Your starter kit

  • RIDGID 5 gallon wet/dry shop vac — ~$119 (Home Depot · See options on Amazon)
  • Heavy-duty contractor trash bags and work gloves — price varies (any hardware store · See options on Amazon)

The post's ~$140 total is the shop vac plus heavy trash bags and gloves. If you also do Move-Out Cleaning, it's the same shop vac.

What customers pay pros

Professional garage cleanouts average $150 to $500 for a 1-car garage and $250 to $800 for a 2-car garage. (HomeGuide)

As a beginner, quote below that range until you have reviews and photos of your work.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Do a free or discounted cleanout for a friend or relative in exchange for before/after photos and a review.
  2. Post the transformation in local groups in spring and fall, when people tackle garages.
  3. Partner with real estate agents: sellers often need garages cleared before listing photos.
Watch out: lift with your legs and get help with heavy items. Old paint, chemicals, batteries and propane tanks can't go in regular trash, so check your town's hazardous waste drop-off rules. Agree up front who hauls items away; large haul-aways may need a truck or a junk service.

Trash Bin Cleaning

~$230 to start

You wash, degrease and deodorize customers' curbside trash and recycling bins right after pickup day, when they're empty. Most customers sign up for a monthly or quarterly cleaning.

Good fit if: you want a simple route-based service and don't mind smelly work. If you already own a pressure washer for Idea 01, your starting cost drops a lot.

Your starter kit

  • Briggs & Stratton 2000 PSI electric pressure washer — ~$195 (Home Depot · See options on Amazon)
  • Degreaser and a stiff scrub brush — price varies (any hardware store · See options on Amazon)

The post's ~$230 total is the pressure washer plus degreaser and a stiff brush.

What to charge

We could not find a reliable national price guide for this one. Search your ZIP code on Thumbtack or Nextdoor and price yourself just under local listings.

Landing your first 3 customers

  1. Clean your own bins and your next-door neighbors' for free on pickup day, and take before/after photos of the inside.
  2. Leave a door hanger on every house on your street with your price and the day you'll come by (the day after pickup).
  3. Offer a small discount when three or more neighbors on one street sign up, so your route is quick.
Watch out: dirty bin water shouldn't go into storm drains, so check your city's rules on where wash water can go before you start. Wear gloves and eye protection, and ask permission before using a customer's outdoor spigot.

Before you take your first job

  1. Check local rules. Search "[your city] business license" to see whether you need one. Rules vary by city and state, and some places also regulate where wash water can drain.
  2. Think about insurance. Ask a local agent about general liability insurance before ladder, pressure washing or in-home work; one accident can cost more than a season's income.
  3. Set clear prices. Write a simple price list (per driveway, per story, per visit) and give written quotes by text so there are no surprises.
  4. Take payment simply. Use a payment app you already have and send a receipt for every job.
  5. Track every dollar. Keep receipts for tools and supplies; side income is taxable, so set some aside.
Pro tip

Send a before and after photo with every quote. It does the selling for you.

FAQ

Which of these is cheapest to start?

Pet waste removal, at about $30 for a pan-and-rake scooper and sturdy bags. Gutter cleaning and garage cleanouts are next at about $140 each.

Do I need a business license?

It depends on your city and state. Many places require a general business license for paid services, even part-time. Search your city's website or call the clerk's office before you start.

How much can I make?

We don't promise earnings. The ranges above are what customers typically pay professionals, from published cost guides. As a beginner, expect to charge less until you have reviews and photos of your work.

Can I combine a few of these?

Yes. Pressure washing and trash bin cleaning share the same washer, and move-out cleaning and garage cleanouts share the same shop vac, so adding a second service costs little.

Can teens do these?

Pet waste removal and trash bin cleaning are good starter jobs for teens with a parent's OK. Skip ladder work and pressure washers until you're older, and check your state's rules for working minors.
